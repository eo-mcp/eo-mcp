Architecture & Data Ingestion

How autonomous agents interact with multi-petabyte government satellite archives without local storage overhead.

eo-mcp implements the Model Context Protocol (MCP) using standard JSON-RPC 2.0 messages over standard I/O (stdio) or Server-Sent Events (SSE). When an AI model (Claude, Cursor, Codex, Antigravity) reasons about an Earth science query, it generates structured tool calls that the server routes to cloud-native endpoints.

Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFF (COG) Range Streaming

Traditional geospatial software downloads entire satellite granules (typically 800 MB to 1.5 GB per scene). When querying multiple sensors or historical baselines, bandwidth constraints and disk input/output cause catastrophic latency for agentic workflows.

eo-mcp bypasses full granule ingestion entirely by utilizing HTTP GET Range requests (via GDAL virtual file system /vsicurl/ and rasterio). A typical query executes in four steps:

  1. Metadata Header Parse: Reads the initial 10 KB to 16 KB of the COG header to retrieve the image dimensions, tile index offsets, and coordinate reference system (CRS).
  2. Coordinate Window Transform: Projects the agent's WGS84 bounding box ([min_lon, min_lat, max_lon, max_lat]) into pixel window offsets corresponding to internal COG tiles.
  3. Targeted Byte Range Retrieval: Fetches only the discrete byte ranges containing the required pixel clusters across individual spectral bands.
  4. Vectorized In-Memory Computation: Decodes pixel blocks into NumPy float32 arrays, applies physical calibration factors, executes band math, and returns statistical metrics and GeoTIFF assets.

Bandwidth Reduction: Reduces payload from ~1,100 MB down to 2.8 MB (a 98.7% bandwidth reduction) while completing full spectral index computations in under two seconds.

1. Optical & Multispectral Biophysical Tools

Planetary-scale vegetation health, canopy structure, surface moisture, and multi-temporal agricultural dynamics.

Zero-Config calculate_spectral_index
Sentinel-2 L2A & Landsat 8/9 C2

Streams calibrated surface reflectance bands from open Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs and evaluates mathematical index formulas. Supports vegetation vigor, canopy moisture, soil composition, and burn characteristics.

Radiative Transfer Science & Spectral Signatures

Photosynthetically active vegetation exhibits a pronounced spectral signature: strong absorption in the red chlorophyll absorption band (0.64 - 0.67 µm) coupled with high scattering in the Near-Infrared (NIR) leaf mesophyll plateau (0.83 - 0.88 µm). This sharp transition (the "red edge") allows quantitative separation of green biomass from bare soil, rock, and water bodies.

NDVI = (NIR - Red) / (NIR + Red)
Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (Rouse et al., 1974). Sentinel-2: (B08 - B04) / (B08 + B04); Landsat 8/9: (B05 - B04) / (B05 + B04). Dynamic range: -1.0 to +1.0. Dense healthy canopy values: 0.60 to 0.85.
NDWI = (Green - NIR) / (Green + NIR)
Normalized Difference Water Index (McFeeters, 1996). Sentinel-2: (B03 - B08) / (B03 + B08). Open water features yield positive values due to high green reflectance and near-total NIR absorption.
EVI = 2.5 * ((NIR - Red) / (NIR + 6.0 * Red - 7.5 * Blue + 1.0))
Enhanced Vegetation Index (Huete et al., 2002). Incorporates atmospheric blue scattering correction and a canopy background adjustment factor (L=1.0) to eliminate saturation over dense tropical rainforests.
MNDWI = (Green - SWIR) / (Green + SWIR)
Modified NDWI (Xu, 2006). Uses Shortwave Infrared (SWIR1: 1.61 µm) to suppress false water signals from urban concrete and built-up land.

Zero-Config monitor_crop_phenology
Sentinel-2 Multi-Temporal Dense Trajectories

Constructs a chronological time series of vegetation indices across agricultural seasons to identify key biological milestones: green-up, maximum photosynthetic activity, senescence, and harvest timing.

Phenological Curve Fitting & Agricultural Milestones

Vegetation growth exhibits an asymmetric sigmoidal trajectory. eo-mcp calculates seasonal baseline amplitude:

A = NDVI_{max} - NDVI_{min}
Seasonal vegetative dynamic range. Threshold for transition detection is calibrated to 20% amplitude: \(T_{20} = NDVI_{min} + 0.20 \cdot A\).
  • Start of Season (SOS): Calendar date where rising spring NDVI crosses the 20% threshold, denoting leaf emergence or crop germination.
  • Peak of Season (POS): The apex of photosynthetic activity (\(NDVI_{max}\)), indicating full canopy closure.
  • End of Season (EOS): Date where post-peak NDVI drops below the 20% threshold due to crop ripening, senescence, or harvesting.
  • Length of Season (LOS): Total duration in days between SOS and EOS (\(LOS = t_{EOS} - t_{SOS}\)).
  • Integrated Seasonal NDVI: Time-integral representing net primary agricultural biomass productivity, aligned with European Common Agricultural Policy (CAP) monitoring mandates.

Zero-Config analyze_reservoir_drought
EC JRC Surface Water Dynamics

Evaluates surface water shrinkage in inland lakes, reservoirs, and wetlands across multi-year baselines, tracking transitions between permanent and seasonal water bodies to classify drought severity.

Hydrological Depletion & Surface Water Transition

Water body area is extracted by segmenting multi-temporal MNDWI layers using bimodal Otsu thresholding. By comparing a historical baseline year (\(t_0\)) to modern acquisitions (\(t_1\)), the tool delineates surface water dynamics:

\Delta A_{water} = A_{recent} - A_{historical}; \quad Deficit\% = \frac{|\Delta A_{water}|}{A_{historical}} \times 100
Quantifies volumetric shrinkage proxy. Classifies depletion according to European Drought Observatory (EDO) severity tiers (Normal, Watch, Warning, Alert).

2. Thermal Infrared & Wildfire Analytics

Radiative transfer equations, calibrated Land Surface Temperature, and active thermal anomaly detection.

Zero-Config analyze_urban_heat_island
Landsat 8/9 TIRS Band 10 (10.895 µm)

Extracts calibrated Land Surface Temperature (LST in Celsius) from thermal infrared radiance and computes Urban Heat Island (UHI) thermal anomaly intensities relative to surrounding rural vegetative buffers.

Planck Inverse Law & Emissivity Threshold Modeling

Thermal infrared detectors measure spectral radiance (\(L_\lambda\)) at the top of the atmosphere. Deriving physical surface temperature requires atmospheric and surface emissivity corrections:

T_B = \frac{K_2}{\ln\left(\frac{K_1}{L_\lambda} + 1\right)}
At-satellite Brightness Temperature in Kelvin. Landsat 8/9 TIRS Band 10 calibration constants: \(K_1 = 774.8853 \, \text{W}/(\text{m}^2 \cdot \text{sr} \cdot \mu\text{m})\), \(K_2 = 1321.0789 \, \text{K}\).
P_v = \left(\frac{NDVI - NDVI_{soil}}{NDVI_{veg} - NDVI_{soil}}\right)^2
Fractional Vegetation Cover (\(P_v\) or FVC). Calibrated using global boundary thresholds: \(NDVI_{soil} = 0.20\), \(NDVI_{veg} = 0.50\).
\varepsilon = 0.970 + 0.018 \cdot P_v \quad (\text{for } 0.20 \le NDVI \le 0.50)
Land Surface Emissivity (Sobrino et al., 2004). Pure water: \(\varepsilon = 0.995\); bare soil: \(\varepsilon = 0.960\); dense vegetative canopy: \(\varepsilon = 0.985\).
LST = \frac{T_B}{1 + \left(\frac{\lambda \cdot T_B}{\rho}\right) \ln(\varepsilon)} - 273.15
Final Land Surface Temperature in degrees Celsius. Effective wavelength \(\lambda = 10.895 \, \mu\text{m}\); constant \(\rho = h \cdot c / \sigma = 1.438 \times 10^{-2} \, \text{m} \cdot \text{K}\).

Urban Heat Island Intensity (\(\Delta T\)): Evaluated by establishing a rural buffer perimeter (\(NDVI > 0.40\)) and computing \(\Delta T = LST_{pixel} - \overline{LST}_{rural}\). Areas with \(\Delta T > 4.0^\circ\text{C}\) are flagged as severe microclimate vulnerability zones under EU Climate Adaptation benchmarks.

Zero-Config calculate_burn_severity
USGS FIREMON / CBI Standards

Quantifies wildfire burn severity, biomass consumption, and soil scorch levels by computing pre-fire vs post-fire differenced Normalized Burn Ratios (dNBR and RdNBR).

Differenced Burn Ratio & USGS CBI Stratification

Active healthy vegetation reflects strongly in NIR and absorbs SWIR. Wildfire combustion removes leaf canopy (collapsing NIR reflectance) and exposes dry charcoal, ash, and bare soil (elevating SWIR reflectance).

NBR = \frac{\rho_{NIR} - \rho_{SWIR2}}{\rho_{NIR} + \rho_{SWIR2}}; \quad dNBR = (NBR_{pre} - NBR_{post}) \times 1000
Normalized Burn Ratio Difference (Key & Benson, 2006). Sentinel-2: B08 and B12; Landsat 8/9: B05 and B07.
RdNBR = \frac{dNBR}{\sqrt{|NBR_{pre} / 1000|}}
Relativized dNBR (Miller & Thode, 2007). Normalizes for pre-fire vegetative density, enabling reliable burn severity comparison in semi-arid shrublands.

Severity Tiers (USGS / European Forest Fire Information System EFFIS):

  • < +100: Unburned / post-fire vegetation regrowth.
  • +100 to +269: Low Severity (surface scorch, canopy intact).
  • +270 to +439: Moderate-Low Severity (needle scorch, shrub mortality).
  • +440 to +659: Moderate-High Severity (heavy canopy mortality).
  • > +660: High Severity (complete tree mortality, soil mineral breakdown).
Zero-Config detect_active_wildfires
NASA FIRMS (VIIRS 375m & MODIS 1km)

Connects directly to open NASA FIRMS active fire feeds to retrieve near-real-time thermal hotspots, brightness temperatures, and Fire Radiative Power (FRP in Megawatts), automatically clustering active fire fronts using spatial proximity algorithms.

Wien's Displacement Law & Fire Radiative Power

According to Wien's Displacement Law (\(\lambda_{max} = b / T\)), active flaming combustion (800 K to 1200 K) shifts peak thermal emission from ambient thermal wavelengths (~10 µm) into the mid-wave infrared (MWIR: 3.7 µm - 4.0 µm, VIIRS Band I4).

FRP = A_{pixel} \cdot \sigma_{MWIR} \cdot (T_{fire}^4 - T_{background}^4) \quad [\text{in Megawatts, MW}]
Fire Radiative Power (Wooster et al., 2005). Measures the instantaneous rate of convective and radiative thermal energy release, directly proportional to biomass combustion rates (kg/sec).

3. Radar & Maritime Intelligence (SAR)

Synthetic Aperture Radar physics, radar cross-section backscatter, and dark vessel identification.

Zero-Config detect_water_sar
Sentinel-1 C-Band SAR (5.405 GHz)

Performs cloud-penetrating, day-and-night surface water delineation using Sentinel-1 C-Band Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) Ground Range Detected (GRD) backscatter.

Specular Reflection vs Rough Surface Backscatter

Radar sensors transmit active microwave pulses and record the energy backscattered to the antenna (\(\sigma^0\), sigma nought in decibels, dB).

  • Smooth Open Water: Behaves as a specular reflector. The incoming microwave pulse bounces away from the sensor at an equal and opposite angle, producing an absence of return signal (\(\sigma^0 \le -16.0 \, \text{dB}\) to \(-22.0 \, \text{dB}\), appearing dark in radar imagery).
  • Rough Vegetated Terrain: Produces diffuse volume scattering (\(\sigma^0 \approx -8.0 \, \text{dB}\) to \(-12.0 \, \text{dB}\)).
  • Built Structures & Ships: Act as dihedral corner reflectors, bouncing signals directly back to the antenna (\(\sigma^0 \ge 0.0 \, \text{dB}\) to \(+15.0 \, \text{dB}\), appearing intensely bright).

Because C-band microwaves (wavelength \(\lambda = 5.6 \, \text{cm}\)) pass through cloud droplets, precipitation, and smoke unimpeded, detect_water_sar provides all-weather flood mapping when optical imagery is blinded.

Zero-Config detect_dark_vessels
Sentinel-1 SAR CFAR vs Open AIS Feeds

Applies Constant False Alarm Rate (CFAR) radar detectors to extract metallic vessel targets from Sentinel-1 SAR imagery and cross-references their spatial positions against live open AIS transponder records to flag unreported "Dark Vessels".

Constant False Alarm Rate (CFAR) Radar Detection

In dynamic ocean environments, sea clutter varies with wind speed and wave swell. Rather than using a static threshold, CFAR dynamically samples a background sea clutter ring around each candidate pixel:

T = \mu_{clutter} + \alpha \cdot \sigma_{clutter}
Cell-Averaging CFAR threshold equation. Guard cells prevent vessel target energy from corrupting background clutter mean (\(\mu\)) and standard deviation (\(\sigma\)). Factor \(\alpha\) guarantees an invariant false alarm probability (\(P_{fa}\)).

Candidate targets with Radar Cross Section (RCS) exceeding threshold \(T\) are measured for spatial elongation (estimated vessel length in meters). The server then queries open maritime AIS transponder feeds (e.g. Digitraffic Baltic open feeds). Radar targets lacking an AIS broadcast within a 2.5 km spatial tolerance are flagged as Unreported Dark Vessels, directly supporting maritime safety, border surveillance, and illegal fishing (IUU) enforcement.

4. Coastal Dynamics, Inundation & Terrain

Sub-pixel shoreline extraction, Digital Shoreline Analysis System transect change rates, and hydrologic flood routing.

Zero-Config analyze_coastal_erosion
MNDWI Otsu Waterlines & DSAS Transects

Quantifies coastal shoreline retreat and accretion rates (in meters/year) between historical and modern satellite observations using automated bimodal Otsu waterline segmentation and perpendicular cross-shore transects.

Digital Shoreline Analysis System (DSAS) Methodology

Shoreline extraction begins by computing Modified Normalized Difference Water Index (MNDWI) rasters. An automated Otsu threshold separates land from water by maximizing inter-class variance (\(\sigma_B^2\)):

\sigma_B^2(t) = \omega_0(t)\omega_1(t) \left[\mu_0(t) - \mu_1(t)\right]^2
Otsu bimodal segmentation. Maximizes between-class variance between water and land reflectance clusters.

Following morphological boundary thinning, cross-shore transects are cast perpendicular to an inland baseline at regular intervals (e.g. 50m). Shoreline change is calculated via the End Point Rate (EPR):

EPR = \frac{D_{recent} - D_{baseline}}{\Delta t} \quad [\text{in meters/year}]
Negative EPR values indicate coastal shoreline erosion and land loss; positive values denote sediment accretion.
Zero-Config simulate_sea_level_rise
Copernicus DEM GLO-30 & IPCC AR6

Simulates coastal sea-level rise and storm surge inundation on Copernicus DEM GLO-30 elevation data, applying hydrologic connectivity algorithms to eliminate false positive flooding in isolated inland depressions.

8-Neighbor Hydrologic Flood-Fill & IPCC AR6 Projections

Simple "bathtub" elevation slicing produces major inaccuracies by classifying inland dry valleys below sea level as submerged, even when protected by natural coastal ridges. eo-mcp enforces an 8-connected hydrologic flood-fill algorithm:

  1. Initializes open oceanic boundary seed pixels at datum \(z \le 0.0 \, \text{m}\).
  2. Simulates total elevated water surface: \(Z_{water} = Z_{SLR} + Z_{surge}\).
  3. Recursively propagates water into adjacent terrain cells only if elevation \(z \le Z_{water}\) AND a contiguous 8-connected water path to the sea exists.
D_{inundation} = \max(0, Z_{water} - z_{DEM}) \quad [\text{depth in meters}]
Water depth grid. Evaluates total submerged area (hectares, km²) and hazard zoning under EU Floods Directive (2007/60/EC Art. 6).

Built-in IPCC Sixth Assessment Report (AR6) scenarios:

  • SSP1-2.6: Sustainability scenario (+0.44m median global SLR by 2100).
  • SSP2-4.5: Intermediate emissions scenario (+0.56m median global SLR by 2100).
  • SSP5-8.5: High emissions fossil-fueled scenario (+0.77m median global SLR by 2100).
Zero-Config get_elevation_profile
Copernicus DEM GLO-30 (30m Global DSM)

Extracts topographic elevation, terrain slope gradients, and aspect orientation from the gold-standard Copernicus Digital Elevation Model (GLO-30) without local raster downloads.

5. Atmospheric & Trace Gas Telemetry

Differential optical absorption spectroscopy and urban air quality directive compliance.

Zero-Config monitor_atmospheric_emissions
Sentinel-5P TROPOMI & OpenAQ Validation

Queries Copernicus Sentinel-5P TROPOMI spectrometer STAC endpoints to extract tropospheric vertical column densities for Nitrogen Dioxide (NO₂), Sulfur Dioxide (SO₂), Carbon Monoxide (CO), and Methane (CH₄), correlating space-based observations with ground-level OpenAQ stations.

Differential Optical Absorption Spectroscopy (DOAS)

The TROPOMI instrument (TROPOspheric Monitoring Instrument) aboard Sentinel-5P measures ultraviolet, visible, near-infrared, and shortwave infrared backscatter with a daily global revisit.

DOAS isolates trace gas absorption cross-sections from broad atmospheric Rayleigh and aerosol scattering, yielding Tropospheric Vertical Column Densities (\(VCD\) in \(\mu\text{mol}/\text{m}^2\) or \(\text{mol}/\text{m}^2\)):

VCD = \frac{SCD}{AMF}
Converts Slant Column Density (\(SCD\)) to Vertical Column Density (\(VCD\)) using an Air Mass Factor (\(AMF\)) derived from radiative transfer models of viewing geometry, surface albedo, and cloud fractions.

Regulatory Thresholds Aligned:

  • NO₂: Clean baseline: < 40 µmol/m²; Elevated urban traffic: 85 µmol/m²; Industrial plume: > 150 µmol/m² (EU Directive 2008/50/EC).
  • CH₄ (Methane): Clean: ~1,850 ppb; Critical super-emitter plume: > 2,000 ppb (EU Methane Regulation 2024/1787).
  • SO₂: Volcanic and coal power emission threshold: > 50 µmol/m² (Industrial Emissions Directive 2010/75/EU).

6. Discovery, Geocoding & Sandbox Execution

Spatial indexing, catalog exploration, authenticated egress, and safe arbitrary Python code execution.

Zero-Config eo_geocode
OpenStreetMap Nominatim

Translates natural language place names (e.g. "Gulf of Corinth", "Valencia, Spain", "Mount Saint Helens") into standardized WGS84 bounding boxes [min_lon, min_lat, max_lon, max_lat] required by all downstream satellite processing tools.

Zero-Config stac_search
Spatio-Temporal Asset Catalog (STAC) Spec

Executes spatio-temporal catalog queries across open STAC endpoints (AWS Earth Search, NASA CMR, Copernicus CDSE). Returns scene IDs, acquisition dates, cloud coverage metrics, and direct Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFF asset links.

Sandbox run_geospatial_script
Isolated Python Engine

Allows autonomous coding agents to execute customized Python scripts inside an isolated runtime pre-loaded with rasterio, numpy, shapely, xarray, and geopandas for specialized mathematical analyses beyond standard tools.

Authentication configure_credentials / download_copernicus_granule
Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem (CDSE) & NASA Earthdata

While standard streaming tools require zero authentication, these tools allow agents to store optional CDSE or NASA OAuth tokens and trigger authenticated full-granule SAFE package downloads directly from official European Space Agency archives.
